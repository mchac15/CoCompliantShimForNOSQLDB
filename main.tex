\section*{System Model}

\paragraph{Failure Model.}
Crash recovery is out of scope. The shim is assumed not to crash: in particular not between voting YES and the coordinator's decision. Nothing is persisted (\texttt{locks\_map}, \texttt{transactions\_map} and the write buffers are volatile), so 2PC atomicity holds only under this no-crash assumption. No Byzantine failures. Network is asynchronous: messages may be delayed, lost, duplicated, reordered, not corrupted; coordinator messages are therefore idempotent at the shim. Partitions eventually heal.

\paragraph{Storage Assumptions.}
The underlying NoSQL store is a black box offering only \texttt{read} and \texttt{write}: \texttt{write} is a blind put and no atomicity across keys is assumed. Store operations are blocking and are retried until they succeed, so the protocol has no error path for store failures.

\paragraph{Deadlocks.}
Every subtransaction has an age \texttt{ts}, taken from its global transaction id, so all shims agree on it; a retry is a new global transaction and is therefore younger. The waits of the execution phase (\Call{lock}{} and \Call{upgrade}{}) follow \emph{wound-wait}: an older subtransaction never waits for a younger one that is still running; it marks it \texttt{must\_abort} and aborts it instead (the victim has not executed, so nobody read its buffer and it voted nothing). A younger subtransaction waits for an older one. Every wait-for edge between running subtransactions thus goes from younger to older, and no cycle can form. With speculation, two shims can also order the same two subtransactions oppositely, and each \texttt{prepare} then waits for the other's commit, i.e.\ for the other's vote. \texttt{prepare} therefore follows \emph{wait-die}: it votes NO instead of waiting for a younger, still undecided predecessor. Since the ages are global, prepare waits too only go from younger to older on every shim. A timeout on the execution-phase waits remains as a backstop only.

\paragraph{Coordinator Assumptions.}
\texttt{txn\_id}s are assigned by the coordinator. It calls \texttt{start} at most once per \texttt{txn\_id}, sends \texttt{end} at most once, and sends no \texttt{get}/\texttt{put}/\texttt{end}/\texttt{prepare}/\texttt{commit} for a \texttt{txn\_id} before its \texttt{start} has been delivered. The \texttt{get}/\texttt{put}/\texttt{end} requests of one subtransaction are sent one at a time, each only after the response to the previous one, so they never overlap and \texttt{end} comes after the last \texttt{get}/\texttt{put} has returned. The shim does not check this: if \texttt{end} overtook a running \texttt{put}, a successor could read the key before the write is in the buffer, and the write could land after the YES vote. The client driver enforces it by serializing the requests of a connection; a client that bypasses the driver is outside this assumption. \texttt{abort}, \texttt{prepare} and \texttt{commit} are not covered and may arrive while a \texttt{get}/\texttt{put} is running. An \texttt{abort}, however, may arrive before the \texttt{start} (e.g.\ a global timeout right after the branch was registered). The shim then records a \emph{tombstone} for that \texttt{txn\_id}, so that the late \texttt{start} fails instead of running, voting YES and holding its locks for a global transaction that was rolled back. The shim drops a subtransaction from \texttt{transactions\_map} once it is committed or aborted, and later or duplicate messages for that \texttt{txn\_id} find no entry (\texttt{prepare} votes NO, \texttt{commit} is a no-op, \texttt{get}/\texttt{put}/\texttt{end} fail, \texttt{abort} leaves a tombstone).

\paragraph{Tombstones.}
A tombstone is an entry of \texttt{transactions\_map} with status \texttt{aborted}, no locks and an expiry time. It is kept in the same map as the subtransactions so that \texttt{abort}'s ``known subtransaction, or else tombstone'' and \texttt{start}'s ``no entry, tombstone included'' are each one atomic operation on the same map entry: either the \texttt{start} registers first and the \texttt{abort} finds and aborts the subtransaction, or the \texttt{abort} leaves its tombstone first and the \texttt{start} finds it and fails. There is no window in which the \texttt{abort} misses the subtransaction and the \texttt{start} misses the tombstone; an implementation with a separate tombstone map must preserve this with a per-\texttt{txn\_id} critical section. Every other request on a tombstoned \texttt{txn\_id} needs no new branch, since it finds an aborted subtransaction without locks: \texttt{prepare} votes NO, \texttt{commit} is a no-op, \texttt{get}/\texttt{put}/\texttt{end} fail. A tombstone is only ever created for a \texttt{txn\_id} without an entry, so it never replaces or aborts a registered subtransaction (\texttt{prepared} stays final), and it sits in no chain, so there is nothing to cascade or splice. Tombstones are garbage-collected by the shim after \texttt{tombstone\_ttl}, to be chosen at least as large as the coordinator's maximum global transaction timeout. This is not definitive: if a \texttt{start} arrives after its tombstone expired, the subtransaction is accepted although the global transaction was rolled back; the TTL only makes this unlikely. A tombstone is never removed by a request. Most never meet a \texttt{start} at all: a retried \texttt{abort} of an already evicted subtransaction, or the \texttt{abort} sent after a failed phase 1 to a shim that voted NO, leave one too, since an evicted \texttt{txn\_id} cannot be told from a never-started one. The TTL is what bounds them.

\paragraph{Execution Model.}
The shim never receives or runs client code: a subtransaction reaches it as a sequence of requests over a connection, \texttt{start(txn\_id)}, then one \texttt{get}/\texttt{put} request per operation, then \texttt{end(txn\_id)} once the client sent its last operation, followed by the coordinator's \texttt{prepare} and \texttt{commit} or \texttt{abort}. Each \texttt{get}/\texttt{put} request acquires a lock on its key dynamically, at the point of access, rather than declaring its full read/write set upfront. A subtransaction proceeds speculatively past a conflicting predecessor once that predecessor reaches \texttt{executed} --- its value is fixed even though undecided --- and only blocks on full resolution at \texttt{prepare}, where it waits until every predecessor has committed or has been removed from the chain by an abort. A waiter never aborts merely because it sees an aborted predecessor: the cascade of the aborting transaction dooms exactly its dependents before it is removed, walking the chain behind each of its writes and stopping at the next blind writer, which did not read the aborted value and whose own successors read the blind write instead. Only the holders that were \emph{granted} (that got past the wait in \Call{lock}{}) are doomed: one still blocked there has read nothing and just keeps waiting. A subtransaction that never reached \texttt{executed} (wounded, or aborted by the coordinator while running) dooms nobody, since no successor can have read its buffer. A later write to an already-read key triggers a lock upgrade: in place if the subtransaction is the sole reader, otherwise onto a new exclusive node behind the readers. An upgrade may move a subtransaction only across readers, never across a writer, since the value it read would be stale by the time its own write takes effect. If a blind writer (one that never read the key) is already queued behind, the new node goes in front of it; if the node behind is itself an upgrade, i.e.\ its holder read the key too, exactly one of the two read-modify-write subtransactions must abort, and it is the later upgrader. The execution phase of a subtransaction spans its requests from \texttt{start} (status \texttt{started}) to \texttt{end}, which flips the status to \texttt{executed} --- but it is not uninterruptible in the scheduling sense: an individual \texttt{get}/\texttt{put} request can block on \Call{lock}{} waiting for another subtransaction, and a \texttt{FAILED} result from any \texttt{get}/\texttt{put} (a \texttt{MUST\_ABORT} from \Call{lock}{} or from \Call{upgrade}{}'s case 3b) means the subtransaction has already been aborted and its locks released, so the client sends no further operations for it. A cascade or a coordinator abort can also land between two requests or after the last one; \texttt{end} then finds the status no longer \texttt{started}, fails its CAS and calls the idempotent \Call{abort\_transaction}{} itself, since no running request would otherwise release the locks. Requests on an unknown or already evicted \texttt{txn\_id} fail, and a \texttt{get}/\texttt{put} arriving after \texttt{end} is refused without touching the subtransaction. The client may not access the store or acquire locks except through \texttt{get}/\texttt{put} requests. Writes are buffered per-transaction (\texttt{write\_buffer}) and applied to the store only at \texttt{commit}, rather than in place at \texttt{put}-time; the buffer is final once the subtransaction is \texttt{executed}. A \texttt{get} first returns the subtransaction's own buffered write to that key, if any. Otherwise, when it is speculating on a predecessor, it reads the value from that predecessor's \texttt{write\_buffer} rather than from the store: it walks back from its own node to the nearest EXCLUSIVE node ahead whose holder wrote the key (never a successor's) and takes the value from that holder's buffer, which is safe because \Call{lock}{} returns only once every predecessor holder is at least \texttt{executed}. If no such node remains, the writer has already committed and applied its buffer to the store, so the store holds the correct value. The value returned is also recorded in a per-transaction \texttt{read\_buffer}, and a repeated \texttt{get} on the same key returns it without walking the chain or reading the store again. The cache needs no invalidation: while the subtransaction holds its lock on the key, the version it may see cannot change, since a predecessor writer that commits leaves the same value in the store, one that aborts dooms the reader, and an upgrade never places a writer in front of an existing reader. A single cache shared by all subtransactions would not work, because readers at different positions in a chain must see different versions. A subtransaction votes YES once every predecessor has resolved and its status has been CASed to \texttt{prepared}. On \texttt{commit} the status moves to \texttt{committing}, the buffer is applied to the store and the locks are released; only then is the status \texttt{committed}. The intermediate \texttt{committing} state keeps a coordinator abort from taking a subtransaction whose commit has been decided, and keeps a successor's prepare from passing before the writes are applied.

\paragraph{Get-for-update.}
\texttt{get\_for\_update(k)} (\texttt{SELECT ... FOR UPDATE}) is a \texttt{get} that takes the EXCLUSIVE lock right away, its node marked as one whose holder read the key. The later \texttt{put(k)} then needs no upgrade, and two read-modify-writes of a key queue one behind the other instead of one of them aborting in \Call{upgrade}{}. Such a holder is not a blind writer: a cascade does not stop at it. A speculative read skips it if it did not write the key.

\paragraph{Fanout Cap.}
Speculation costs cascading aborts. To bound how many subtransactions one abort can doom, each writer may have at most \texttt{fanout\_cap} running \emph{dependents}, i.e.\ subtransactions granted on top of it. A dependent counts once, however many keys it depends on it through. A grant that would exceed the cap waits, as without speculation, until the writer commits or loses dependents. To stay deadlock-free, a refused grant that would wait for a younger subtransaction's commit is taken anyway, past the cap. The cap is the shim's fractional value $F$ (each writer draws $\lfloor F \rfloor$ or $\lfloor F \rfloor + 1$, $F$ on average), tuned at run time by a hill-climb on the shim's commit rate.

\paragraph{Architecture.}
There is one shim per data source, where a data source is a node together with a database name. The shim node is colocated with the store or runs standalone next to it. One server can host several databases, each with its own shim, i.e.\ its own \texttt{locks\_map} and \texttt{transactions\_map}; nothing is shared between shims. A shim manages all the tables of its database. Each shim accepts many concurrent connections, only from the coordinator side (the 2PC participants). A connection carries one subtransaction at a time, while many subtransactions run concurrently on different connections; each request runs on the thread handling its connection and blocks only that thread. A subtransaction is identified by its \texttt{txn\_id}, not by a connection, so its requests may arrive on different connections; that they never overlap comes from the coordinator assumptions (one request at a time, enforced by the client driver), not from the connection. Given that, the only foreign threads that touch a running subtransaction are cascades and the coordinator's \texttt{abort}/\texttt{prepare}. A subtransaction is bound to a single data source by definition --- access to several databases is expressed as multiple subtransactions within one Sonata global transaction, coordinated by Sonata's outer 2PC, not by any single subtransaction spanning data sources. The shim's \texttt{locks\_map} is therefore scoped to one data source, with a single point of serialization; no cross-node lock coordination is needed within the shim itself.

\paragraph{Data Model.}
The shim locks at the granularity of a single record, identified by its table and its primary key: a key $k$ in the pseudocode stands for the pair (table, key); this is the unit \texttt{get}/\texttt{put} and the \texttt{locks\_map} chains operate on. Values are treated as opaque --- the shim does not interpret or type-check them, only serializes access to them via SHARED/EXCLUSIVE locks.

\State \textbf{Data Structures:}
\State $\text{lock\_mode} \in \{\text{SHARED}, \text{EXCLUSIVE}\}$
\State $\text{LockNode}: \{\text{mode}, \text{holders}: \text{set of txn}, \text{granted}: \text{set of txn}, \text{predecessor}, \text{next}, \text{upgraded}\}$ \Comment{one generation of holders of a key: consecutive SHARED requests share a node, every EXCLUSIVE request (or a SHARED one behind an EXCLUSIVE node) gets a new one, so an EXCLUSIVE node has one holder}
\State \Comment{granted $\subseteq$ holders: the holders that got past the wait in \Call{lock}{}, i.e.\ that may have read the key; a holder still blocked there read nothing, so a cascade spares it}
\State \Comment{upgraded: the holder read $k$ before writing it (\Call{upgrade}{} or get\_for\_update); a node from a plain \Call{put}{k} is a blind write: it depends on no earlier value of $k$}
\State $\text{chain}: \{\text{head}, \text{tail}\}$ \Comment{doubly linked through predecessor/next; order $=$ required commit order}
\State $\text{locks\_map}\colon \texttt{ConcurrentHashMap<key, chain>}$ \Comment{one chain per key; a key $k$ stands for (table, key)}
\State $\text{txn}: \{\text{txn\_id}, \text{ts}, \text{status}, \text{locks\_acquired}: \text{key} \to (\text{mode}, \text{node}), \text{write\_buffer}: \text{key} \to \text{value}, \text{read\_buffer}: \text{key} \to \text{value}, \text{reached\_executed}, \text{depends\_on}: \text{set of txn}, \text{dependents}, \text{fanout\_cap}\}$
\State \Comment{ts: the txn's age (smaller $=$ older), taken from the global transaction id, so every shim agrees on it; a retry is a new global transaction, hence younger}
\State \Comment{read\_buffer: the value each \Call{get}{} returned, so a repeated \Call{get}{k} returns the same value}
\State \Comment{reached\_executed: the txn was executed at some point, so a successor may have read its write\_buffer}
\State \Comment{depends\_on / dependents / fanout\_cap: the fanout cap's bookkeeping (see \Call{admit\_fanout}{})}
\State $\text{status} \in \{\text{started}, \text{executed}, \text{prepared}, \text{committing}, \text{committed}, \text{aborted}, \text{must\_abort}\}$
\State $\text{SPEC\_READY} = \{\text{executed}, \text{prepared}, \text{committing}, \text{committed}\}$ \Comment{the speculation point: from executed on, a txn's write\_buffer no longer changes, so successors may read on top of it}
\State \Comment{the non-speculative baseline is the same protocol with $\text{SPEC\_READY} = \{\text{committed}\}$ (no successor ever reads an uncommitted value, no abort ever cascades, no fanout cap)}
\State $\text{transactions\_map}\colon \texttt{ConcurrentHashMap<txn\_id, txn>}$ \Comment{also holds the tombstones: an aborted txn with no locks and an expires\_at, left by an abort that came before the start (see \Call{abort}{}), removed after tombstone\_ttl}
\State $F$: real $\geq 0$ \Comment{the shim's adaptive fanout cap, tuned by \Call{fanout\_controller}{}}
\State $\text{lock\_timeout}$ \Comment{backstop on the waits of \Call{lock}{}/\Call{upgrade}{}; deadlocks are prevented by wound-wait}
\State \Comment{atomic(locks\_map) is one critical section over the chains (an implementation may use a latch per key, one at a time); ``wait until'' sleeps until something changes on the chain it reads}

\Statex
\State \textbf{Store:}
\State \Call{store.read}{k} $\to$ value
\State \Call{store.write}{k, v} \Comment{blind put, blocking, no atomicity across keys}
\State \textbf{Assumptions:}
\State \Comment{1. No crashes: crash recovery is out of scope (nothing is logged).}
\State \Comment{2. The coordinator assigns txn\_ids, calls start once per txn\_id, and sends get/put/end of one txn one at a time (the client driver serializes them). abort/prepare/commit may arrive at any time, an abort even before the start (the shim then leaves a tombstone).}
\State \Comment{3. Deadlocks: execution-phase waits are made acyclic by wound-wait, prepare-phase waits by wait-die (both on the global ages ts). lock\_timeout only remains as a backstop.}

\Statex
\Procedure{start}{txn\_id}
    \State $txn \gets$ new txn with $\text{txn\_id}$, $\text{ts} = \Call{age}{txn\_id}$, $\text{status}=\text{started}$, everything else empty
    \If{$\Call{transactions\_map.putIfAbsent}{txn\_id, txn} \neq \text{null}$}
        \State \textbf{return} FAILED \Comment{duplicate, or tombstoned: the coordinator already aborted this id}
    \EndIf
    \State \textbf{return} SUCCEEDED
\EndProcedure

\Statex
\Procedure{end}{txn\_id} \Comment{the client sent its last get/put: the write\_buffer is final from here on}
    \State $txn \gets \text{transactions\_map}[txn\_id]$
    \If{$txn == \text{null}$}
        \State \textbf{return} FAILED
    \EndIf
    \If{\textbf{not} \Call{CAS}{txn.\text{status}, \text{started}, \text{executed}}}
        \If{$txn.\text{status} \in \{\text{executed}, \text{prepared}, \text{committing}, \text{committed}\}$}
            \State \textbf{return} SUCCEEDED \Comment{duplicate end}
        \EndIf
        \State \Call{abort\_transaction}{txn} \Comment{doomed while running: release its locks (idempotent)}
        \State \textbf{return} FAILED
    \EndIf
    \State $txn.\text{reached\_executed} \gets \text{true}$
    \State \textbf{return} SUCCEEDED
\EndProcedure

\Statex
\Procedure{get}{txn\_id, k, for\_update $=$ false}
    \State \Comment{for\_update $=$ get\_for\_update($k$), i.e.\ SELECT ... FOR UPDATE: the txn will write $k$ after reading it, so it takes the EXCLUSIVE lock right away. The later \Call{put}{k} then needs no upgrade, and two read-modify-writes of $k$ queue one behind the other instead of one of them aborting in \Call{upgrade}{}}
    \State $txn \gets \text{transactions\_map}[txn\_id]$
    \If{$txn == \text{null}$ \textbf{or} $txn.\text{status} \in \{\text{executed}, \text{prepared}, \text{committing}, \text{committed}\}$}
        \State \textbf{return NULL, FAILED} \Comment{unknown txn\_id, or a request after \Call{end}{}}
    \EndIf
    \If{$for\_update$}
        \State $result \gets$ \Call{lock}{k, \text{EXCLUSIVE}, txn, reads\_key $=$ true}
    \Else
        \State $result \gets$ \Call{lock}{k, \text{SHARED}, txn}
    \EndIf
    \If{$result == \text{MUST\_ABORT}$}
        \State \Call{abort\_transaction}{txn}
        \State \textbf{return NULL, FAILED}
    \EndIf
    \If{$k \in txn.\text{write\_buffer}$}
        \State \textbf{return} $txn.\text{write\_buffer}[k]$, SUCCEEDED \Comment{own write first}
    \EndIf
    \State $value \gets \text{null}$ \Comment{null $=$ no predecessor version (values are non-null)}
    \State \textbf{atomic}(locks\_map) \{
    \If{$txn.\text{status} \in \{\text{aborted}, \text{must\_abort}\}$}
        \State \indent $doomed \gets \text{true}$ \Comment{a foreign abort released this txn's locks since \Call{lock}{} returned}
    \ElsIf{$k \in txn.\text{read\_buffer}$}
        \State \indent $value \gets txn.\text{read\_buffer}[k]$ \Comment{the version visible to txn cannot change while it holds $k$}
    \Else
        \State \indent \Comment{speculative read: the write of the nearest EXCLUSIVE node ahead of txn's own node whose holder wrote $k$ (a get\_for\_update holder that did not write $k$ holds no version of it). Its write\_buffer is final: \Call{lock}{} only returned once every holder ahead was executed}
        \For{$n \gets txn.\text{locks\_acquired}[k].\text{node.predecessor}$; $n \neq \text{null}$; $n \gets n.\text{predecessor}$}
            \If{$n.\text{mode} == \text{EXCLUSIVE}$ \textbf{and} $k \in (\text{the holder of } n).\text{write\_buffer}$}
                \State \indent $value \gets (\text{the holder of } n).\text{write\_buffer}[k]$; \textbf{break}
            \EndIf
        \EndFor
    \EndIf
    \State \}
    \If{$doomed$}
        \State \Call{abort\_transaction}{txn}
        \State \textbf{return NULL, FAILED}
    \EndIf
    \If{$value == \text{null}$}
        \State $value \gets$ \Call{store.read}{k} \Comment{no uncommitted version ahead: the store holds the latest committed one}
    \EndIf
    \State $txn.\text{read\_buffer}[k] \gets value$
    \State \textbf{return} $value$, SUCCEEDED
\EndProcedure

\Statex
\Procedure{put}{txn\_id, k, v}
    \State $txn \gets \text{transactions\_map}[txn\_id]$
    \If{$txn == \text{null}$ \textbf{or} $txn.\text{status} \in \{\text{executed}, \text{prepared}, \text{committing}, \text{committed}\}$}
        \State \textbf{return FAILED}
    \EndIf
    \If{\Call{lock}{k, \text{EXCLUSIVE}, txn} $== \text{MUST\_ABORT}$}
        \State \Call{abort\_transaction}{txn}
        \State \textbf{return FAILED}
    \EndIf
    \State $txn.\text{write\_buffer}[k] \gets v$
    \State \textbf{return SUCCEEDED}
\EndProcedure

\end{algorithmic}

\begin{algorithmic}[1]

\Procedure{lock}{k, mode, txn, reads\_key $=$ false}
    \State $held \gets txn.\text{locks\_acquired}[k]$
    \If{$held \neq \text{null}$}
        \If{$held.\text{mode} == \text{EXCLUSIVE}$ \textbf{or} $mode == \text{SHARED}$}
            \State \textbf{return} ALREADY\_HELD \textbf{if} $txn.\text{status} == \text{started}$ \textbf{else} MUST\_ABORT
        \EndIf
        \State \textbf{return} \Call{upgrade}{k, txn} \Comment{holds $k$ SHARED, wants it EXCLUSIVE}
    \EndIf
    \State \textbf{atomic}(locks\_map) \{
        \If{$txn.\text{status} \neq \text{started}$}
            \State \indent \textbf{return} MUST\_ABORT \Comment{checked under the latch: a foreign \Call{abort\_transaction}{} may have released txn's locks, and a node appended now would never be removed}
        \EndIf
        \State \indent $chain \gets \text{locks\_map}[k]$, created if absent
        \If{$mode == \text{SHARED}$ \textbf{and} $chain.\text{tail} \neq \text{null}$ \textbf{and} $chain.\text{tail.mode} == \text{SHARED}$}
            \State \indent $node \gets chain.\text{tail}$; $node.\text{holders.add}(txn)$
        \Else
            \State \indent $node \gets \text{LockNode}\{mode, \text{holders}=\{txn\}, \text{granted}=\{\}, \text{upgraded}=reads\_key\}$
            \State \indent append $node$ to $chain$
        \EndIf
        \State \indent $txn.\text{locks\_acquired}[k] \gets (mode, node)$
    \State \}
    \State \textbf{return} \Call{await\_grant}{k, txn, node}
\EndProcedure

\Statex
\Procedure{upgrade}{k, txn} \Comment{SHARED $\to$ EXCLUSIVE; invariant: txn only moves across readers, never across a writer (its read would be stale by the time its own write takes effect: lost update)}
    \State \textbf{atomic}(locks\_map) \{
        \If{$txn.\text{status} \neq \text{started}$}
            \State \indent \textbf{return} MUST\_ABORT
        \EndIf
        \State \indent $node \gets txn.\text{locks\_acquired}[k].\text{node}$; $p \gets node$
        \While{$p.\text{next} \neq \text{null}$ \textbf{and} $p.\text{next.mode} == \text{SHARED}$}
            \State \indent $p \gets p.\text{next}$ \Comment{skip the readers queued behind node}
        \EndWhile
        \State \indent $after \gets p.\text{next}$ \Comment{EXCLUSIVE or null}
        \If{$after \neq \text{null}$ \textbf{and} $after.\text{upgraded}$}
            \State \indent \textbf{return} MUST\_ABORT \Comment{read-modify-write against read-modify-write: this one loses}
        \EndIf
        \If{$p == node$ \textbf{and} $node.\text{holders} == \{txn\}$}
            \State \indent $node.\text{mode} \gets \text{EXCLUSIVE}$; $node.\text{upgraded} \gets \text{true}$ \Comment{sole holder, at most a blind writer behind: flip in place}
            \State \indent $txn.\text{locks\_acquired}[k] \gets (\text{EXCLUSIVE}, node)$
            \State \indent \textbf{return} LOCK\_ACQUIRED
        \EndIf
        \State \indent \Comment{leave node, take a new EXCLUSIVE node right behind $p$: at the tail, or in front of the blind writer $after$ (which then also waits for txn)}
        \State \indent $new\_node \gets \text{LockNode}\{\text{EXCLUSIVE}, \text{holders}=\{txn\}, \text{upgraded}=\text{true}, \text{granted}=\{txn\} \text{ if } txn \in node.\text{granted} \text{ else } \{\}\}$ \Comment{it read $k$: a cascade must reach it}
        \State \indent remove $txn$ from $node.\text{holders}$ (and $node.\text{granted}$); splice $node$ out if it is now empty
        \State \indent insert $new\_node$ between $p$ and $after$
        \State \indent $txn.\text{locks\_acquired}[k] \gets (\text{EXCLUSIVE}, new\_node)$
    \State \}
    \State \textbf{return} \Call{await\_grant}{k, txn, new\_node} \Comment{wait for the readers it was queued behind}
\EndProcedure

\Statex
\Procedure{await\_grant}{k, txn, node}
    \State \Comment{the wait of \Call{lock}{}/\Call{upgrade}{}: until every holder ahead reached the speculation point and the grant fits the fanout cap. Wound-wait keeps it deadlock-free: an older txn never waits for a younger running one, it aborts it instead, so every wait-for edge between running txns goes from younger to older}
    \State $deadline \gets \text{now}() + \text{lock\_timeout}$
    \Loop
        \State $victims \gets \{\}$
        \State \textbf{atomic}(locks\_map) \{
            \If{$txn.\text{status} \in \{\text{aborted}, \text{must\_abort}\}$}
                \State \indent \textbf{return} MUST\_ABORT
            \EndIf
            \State \indent $pred \gets node.\text{predecessor}$ \Comment{re-read every time: nodes ahead are spliced out / inserted meanwhile}
            \If{$pred == \text{null}$ \textbf{or} $\forall t \in pred.\text{holders}: t.\text{status} \in \text{SPEC\_READY}$}
                \If{\Call{admit\_fanout}{txn, node}}
                    \State \indent $node.\text{granted.add}(txn)$
                    \State \indent \textbf{return} LOCK\_ACQUIRED
                \EndIf
                \State \indent \Comment{refused: wait, as without speculation, for the full writer to commit or lose dependents}
            \Else
                \State \indent \Comment{wound-wait: the holders ahead that block txn (not yet executed, not already leaving)}
                \State \indent $blocking \gets \{t \in pred.\text{holders} : t.\text{status} \notin \text{SPEC\_READY} \cup \{\text{must\_abort}, \text{aborted}\}\}$
                \If{$blocking \neq \{\}$ \textbf{and} $\forall t \in blocking: t.\text{status} == \text{started}$ \textbf{and} $t.\text{ts} > txn.\text{ts}$}
                    \For{\textbf{each} $t \in blocking$}
                        \If{\Call{CAS}{t.\text{status}, \text{started}, \text{must\_abort}}} \Comment{a victim is still started: nobody read its buffer, it voted nothing}
                            \State \indent $victims.\text{add}(t)$
                        \EndIf
                    \EndFor
                \EndIf
                \State \indent \Comment{an older holder (or a younger one already executed/prepared) is simply waited for}
            \EndIf
        \State \}
        \If{$victims \neq \{\}$}
            \State \textbf{for each} $t \in victims$\textbf{:} \Call{abort\_transaction}{t} \Comment{outside the latch; its release splices its node out}
            \State \textbf{continue}
        \EndIf
        \State \textbf{wait until} something changes on chain $k$ \textbf{or} $\text{now}() \geq deadline$
        \If{$\text{now}() \geq deadline$}
            \State \textbf{return} MUST\_ABORT \Comment{backstop only (e.g.\ a stalled holder)}
        \EndIf
    \EndLoop
\EndProcedure

\Statex
\Procedure{risky\_ahead}{node} $\to$ list of txn
    \State \Comment{the txns whose abort would cascade to node's holder: the undecided holders of the EXCLUSIVE nodes ahead of it, back to the first blind write (a blind write depends on nobody's value)}
    \If{$node.\text{mode} == \text{EXCLUSIVE}$ \textbf{and not} $node.\text{upgraded}$}
        \State \textbf{return} $\{\}$
    \EndIf
    \State $risky \gets \{\}$
    \For{$n \gets node.\text{predecessor}$; $n \neq \text{null}$; $n \gets n.\text{predecessor}$}
        \If{$n.\text{mode} == \text{EXCLUSIVE}$}
            \State $w \gets$ the holder of $n$
            \If{$w.\text{status} \notin \{\text{committing}, \text{committed}\}$}
                \State $risky.\text{add}(w)$
            \EndIf
            \If{\textbf{not} $n.\text{upgraded}$}
                \State \textbf{break}
            \EndIf
        \EndIf
    \EndFor
    \State \textbf{return} $risky$
\EndProcedure

\Statex
\Procedure{admit\_fanout}{txn, node} $\to$ bool
    \State \Comment{fanout cap: each writer has at most fanout\_cap running dependents, i.e.\ txns whose grant reads on top of it (a dependent counts once, through however many keys). Bounds how many txns one abort can doom. Under the latch, all or nothing: returns true if txn may be granted}
    \State $risky \gets \Call{risky\_ahead}{node} \setminus txn.\text{depends\_on}$
    \For{\textbf{each} $w \in risky$}
        \If{$w.\text{fanout\_cap}$ is unset}
            \State $w.\text{fanout\_cap} \gets \lfloor F \rfloor + (1 \text{ with probability } F - \lfloor F \rfloor)$ \Comment{drawn once: $F$ on average}
        \EndIf
    \EndFor
    \State $full \gets \{w \in risky : w.\text{dependents} \geq w.\text{fanout\_cap}\}$
    \If{$full \neq \{\}$ \textbf{and} $\forall w \in full: w.\text{ts} < txn.\text{ts}$}
        \State \textbf{return} false \Comment{refused: txn waits for an older writer, like without speculation}
    \EndIf
    \State \Comment{admitted, or forced: a refused grant would wait for a younger txn's commit, which may itself wait (in \Call{prepare}{} on another shim) for this txn: a cycle. Then it is granted past the cap}
    \For{\textbf{each} $w \in risky$}
        \State $w.\text{dependents}\texttt{++}$; $txn.\text{depends\_on.add}(w)$
    \EndFor
    \State \textbf{return} true
\EndProcedure

\Statex
\Procedure{release\_dependencies}{txn} \Comment{txn is resolved: it no longer counts as a dependent; wakes the grants refused on those writers}
    \For{\textbf{each} $w \in txn.\text{depends\_on}$}
        \State $w.\text{dependents}\texttt{--}$
    \EndFor
    \State $txn.\text{depends\_on} \gets \{\}$
\EndProcedure

\end{algorithmic}

\begin{algorithmic}[1]

\Procedure{prepare}{txn\_id}
    \State $txn \gets \text{transactions\_map}[txn\_id]$
    \If{$txn == \text{null}$}
        \State \textbf{Send no to 2PC controller}; \textbf{return}
    \EndIf
    \If{$txn.\text{status} \in \{\text{prepared}, \text{committing}, \text{committed}\}$}
        \State \textbf{Send yes to 2PC controller}; \textbf{return} \Comment{duplicate prepare: re-send the vote}
    \EndIf
    \If{$txn.\text{status} \neq \text{executed}$}
        \State \textbf{Send no to 2PC controller} \Comment{still running, aborted or doomed}
        \State \Call{abort\_transaction}{txn}; \textbf{return}
    \EndIf
    \State \Comment{CO: vote only once every txn ahead of txn on every one of its keys has committed}
    \For{\textbf{each} $(k, mode, node) \in txn.\text{locks\_acquired}$}
        \Loop
            \State \textbf{atomic}(locks\_map) \{
                \State \indent $pred \gets node.\text{predecessor}$ \Comment{re-read every time: nodes ahead are spliced out meanwhile}
                \State \indent $ready \gets pred == \text{null}$ \textbf{or} $\forall t \in pred.\text{holders}: t.\text{status} == \text{committed}$
                \State \indent \Comment{wait-die: with speculation two shims can order the same two txns oppositely, and each prepare then waits for the other's commit, i.e.\ for the other's vote. Waiting only for older txns (global ages: the same order on every shim) makes such a cycle impossible. Holders that are committing or already leaving resolve without waiting: waited for}
                \State \indent $die \gets txn.\text{status} \in \{\text{aborted}, \text{must\_abort}\}$ \textbf{or} $\big(\textbf{not } ready$ \textbf{and} $\exists t \in pred.\text{holders}: t.\text{ts} > txn.\text{ts}$ \textbf{and} $t.\text{status} \notin \{\text{committing}, \text{committed}, \text{must\_abort}, \text{aborted}\}\big)$
            \State \}
            \If{$die$}
                \State \textbf{Send no to 2PC controller}
                \State \Call{abort\_transaction}{txn}; \textbf{return}
            \EndIf
            \If{$ready$}
                \State \textbf{break}
            \EndIf
            \State \textbf{wait until} something changes on chain $k$
        \EndLoop
    \EndFor
    \If{\textbf{not} \Call{CAS}{txn.\text{status}, \text{executed}, \text{prepared}}}
        \If{$txn.\text{status} \in \{\text{prepared}, \text{committing}, \text{committed}\}$}
            \State \textbf{Send yes to 2PC controller}; \textbf{return} \Comment{a concurrent duplicate prepare already voted YES}
        \EndIf
        \State \textbf{Send no to 2PC controller}
        \State \Call{abort\_transaction}{txn}; \textbf{return}
    \EndIf
    \State \textbf{Send yes to 2PC controller} \Comment{prepared is final: no cascade or wound can take txn anymore}
\EndProcedure

\Statex
\Procedure{commit}{txn\_id}
    \State $txn \gets \text{transactions\_map}[txn\_id]$
    \If{$txn == \text{null}$ \textbf{or not} \Call{CAS}{txn.\text{status}, \text{prepared}, \text{committing}}}
        \State \textbf{return} \Comment{unknown, duplicate, or a tombstone}
    \EndIf
    \State \Comment{committing: decided, so txn no longer counts in \Call{risky\_ahead}{} (wakes the grants it held back)}
    \For{\textbf{each} $(k, v) \in txn.\text{write\_buffer}$}
        \State \Call{store.write}{k, v}
    \EndFor
    \State \textbf{atomic}(locks\_map) \{
    \For{\textbf{each} $(k, mode, node) \in txn.\text{locks\_acquired}$}
        \State \indent remove $txn$ from $node$; splice $node$ out if it is now empty; remove $k$ from locks\_map if empty
    \EndFor
    \State \}
    \State $txn.\text{status} \gets \text{committed}$
    \State \Call{release\_dependencies}{txn}
    \State $commits\texttt{++}$ \Comment{for \Call{fanout\_controller}{}}
    \State \Call{transactions\_map.remove}{txn\_id}
\EndProcedure

\Statex
\Procedure{abort\_transaction}{txn}
    \If{\textbf{not} \Call{CAS}{txn.\text{status}, \{\text{started}, \text{executed}, \text{must\_abort}, \text{prepared}\}, \text{aborted}}}
        \State \textbf{return} \Comment{already aborted, or committing/committed}
    \EndIf
    \State $doomed\_finished \gets \{\}$
    \State \textbf{atomic}(locks\_map) \{
    \For{\textbf{each} $(k, mode, node) \in txn.\text{locks\_acquired}$}
        \State \indent \Comment{cascade before splice: the dependents are marked before txn leaves the chain, so none of them can get past txn's node and vote YES on its rolled-back write. Only a txn that reached executed can have been read by a successor (\Call{lock}{} waits for that); so a wounded txn, or one the coordinator aborted while it ran, dooms nobody}
        \If{$mode == \text{EXCLUSIVE}$ \textbf{and} $txn.\text{reached\_executed}$}
            \For{$s \gets node.\text{next}$; $s \neq \text{null}$; $s \gets s.\text{next}$}
                \If{$s.\text{mode} == \text{EXCLUSIVE}$ \textbf{and not} $s.\text{upgraded}$}
                    \State \indent \textbf{break} \Comment{blind write: does not depend on txn's value, nor do the nodes behind it}
                \EndIf
                \For{\textbf{each} $t \in s.\text{granted}$} \Comment{holders still blocked in \Call{lock}{} read nothing: spared}
                    \If{\Call{mark\_must\_abort}{t}}
                        \State \indent $doomed\_finished.\text{add}(t)$
                    \EndIf
                \EndFor
            \EndFor
        \EndIf
        \State \indent remove $txn$ from $node$; splice $node$ out if it is now empty; remove $k$ from locks\_map if empty
    \EndFor
    \State \}
    \State \Call{release\_dependencies}{txn}
    \State \Call{transactions\_map.remove}{txn.\text{txn\_id}}
    \State \textbf{for each} $t \in doomed\_finished$\textbf{:} \Call{abort\_transaction}{t} \Comment{a doomed txn that already executed has no thread left to abort it: do it now (cascades transitively)}
\EndProcedure

\Statex
\Procedure{mark\_must\_abort}{txn} $\to$ bool \Comment{never touches prepared (a YES vote is final); a started victim aborts itself at its next request}
    \State $before \gets$ \Call{CAS\_returning}{txn.\text{status}, \{\text{started}, \text{executed}\}, \text{must\_abort}}
    \State \textbf{return} $before == \text{executed}$
\EndProcedure

\Statex
\Procedure{abort}{txn\_id}
    \State \Comment{known txn: abort it. Unknown id: leave a tombstone (status aborted, no locks), atomically with \Call{start}{}'s putIfAbsent, so that a start arriving after the abort fails instead of running}
    \State $txn \gets$ \Call{transactions\_map.computeIfAbsent}{txn\_id, \text{new txn with status} = \text{aborted}, \text{expires\_at} = \text{now}() + \text{tombstone\_ttl}}
    \State \Call{abort\_transaction}{txn} \Comment{a no-op on a tombstone}
\EndProcedure

\Statex
\Procedure{gc\_tombstones}{} \Comment{background}
    \State remove from transactions\_map every tombstone past its expires\_at
\EndProcedure

\Statex
\Procedure{fanout\_controller}{} \Comment{background, one per shim}
    \State \Comment{hill-climbs the fractional fanout cap $F$ on the shim's commit rate. One step changes the rate by less than the noise, so each period is compared with an anchor (the last $F$ that made a clear difference) rather than with the previous period: small effects add up until they show}
    \State $direction \gets$ random $\pm 1$
    \State $anchor\_F, anchor\_rate \gets F$, commit rate of the first period
    \Loop \ \textbf{every} period
        \State $rate \gets$ commits per second over the period, without its first moments (still under the old $F$)
        \If{$rate > anchor\_rate \cdot (1 + \varepsilon)$}
            \State $anchor\_F, anchor\_rate \gets F, rate$; $F \gets \Call{step}{F, direction, \text{big}}$ \Comment{clearly better: go on, faster}
        \ElsIf{$rate < anchor\_rate \cdot (1 - \varepsilon)$}
            \State $F \gets anchor\_F$; $direction \gets -direction$ \Comment{clearly worse: back to the anchor, turn around}
        \ElsIf{too many small steps since the anchor}
            \State $anchor\_F, anchor\_rate \gets F, rate$; $direction \gets -direction$ \Comment{$F$ barely matters here: re-anchor, turn around}
        \Else
            \State $F \gets \Call{step}{F, direction, \text{small}}$ \Comment{no clear effect yet: go on, slowly}
        \EndIf
    \EndLoop
    \State \Comment{step($F, d, s$) $= F \cdot (1 + s)$ if $d > 0$ else $F / (1 + s)$, at most $F_{max}$; below $F_{min}$ it becomes 0 (no speculation but the forced grants), and a step up from 0 goes back to $F_{min}$}
\EndProcedure

\end{algorithmic}
